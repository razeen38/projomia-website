Photo documentation

Construction photo documentation, on your iPhone.

Construction photo documentation means capturing site photos with the context needed to prove progress later: the project, room or area, the date and a note. Projomia is a free iPhone app that files each photo into a project and room, keeps your notes beside it, and stores the whole record in a local library on your phone, even offline.

What good photo documentation captures

  • Milestones. The points that get inspected, questioned or paid for.
  • Work before it is covered. Foundations before backfill, services before walls and ceilings close.
  • Consistent viewpoints. The same spot, the same angle, week after week, so progress is obvious.
  • Context. Project, area, date and a one-line note saying what the photo proves.
  • Problems and their cause. Damage, defects, blocked work and what it is waiting for.

How to document site progress with photos

  1. Set up the project and its areas first. Create the project and the rooms, levels or zones you will photograph, so every picture has somewhere to go.
  2. Decide what and how often. List the milestones and hidden work you must capture, and a regular cadence for general progress (for example, daily walk-round photos of each active area).
  3. Capture into the right place. Take the photo straight into its project and room rather than the camera roll.
  4. Add one sentence. Write what the photo shows and why it matters: “Ceiling closure waiting for AC installation”.
  5. Link photos to the daily record. Refer to them in the day’s daily report so the narrative and the evidence stay together.
  6. Review weekly. Look back by area and date to spot what has stalled, and keep the library backed up.

Stop losing photos in the camera roll and group chats

Site photos sent through messaging apps or left in the camera roll lose their project, location and meaning, and are hard to find when a question comes up months later. Filing each photo into a project and room at the moment you take it, with a note, is the simplest fix.

How Projomia organises site photos

  • Project, room and date. Capture into the project and room you choose, then browse by project, folder and date.
  • Notes beside the evidence. Write the note next to the photo it explains.
  • Drawings and documents. Import images, drawings and documents with the photo picker.
  • Daily Log and Planner. Record daily progress, crew and per-activity progress, plus activities, areas and decisions.
  • Today’s attention. Flag items as blocked, follow-up or decide, each linked to its evidence.

Private by default

Projomia uses the iOS photo picker, so it never has access to your whole photo library. The project library stays in the app on your iPhone, with no Projomia account or server in this release, and capture works without signal. AI is optional and only runs when you connect your own OpenAI account. See the privacy policy.

Questions

What photos should be taken on a construction site?

Milestones, work before it is covered up, the same viewpoints at regular intervals, deliveries, defects and anything blocking progress, each with the date, area and a short note.

How often should construction progress photos be taken?

Set a regular cadence for general progress, such as a daily walk-round of active areas, and always photograph milestones and hidden work before it is closed in.

How do I organise jobsite photos on iPhone?

File each photo into its project and room as you take it and add a one-line note. In Projomia you can then browse by project, folder and date.

Does Projomia upload my site photos anywhere?

No. Photos stay in a local library in the app on your iPhone. There is no Projomia account or server in this release.

Can I add notes to site photos?

Yes. In Projomia the note sits beside the photo it explains, and both can be linked to Daily Log entries and attention items.
