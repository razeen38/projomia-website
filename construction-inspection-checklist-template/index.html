Construction site inspection checklist.

A construction site inspection checklist lists what to check in each area, such as safety, quality, progress and housekeeping, so nothing is missed between visits. Use the free template below, or keep inspection checklists by location in Projomia, a free iPhone app that keeps photos and notes beside each check and works offline.

What a site inspection checklist covers

  • Safety. Access, working at height, excavations, electrics, plant, PPE, fire and emergency.
  • Quality and workmanship. Work matches drawings and specification; defects recorded.
  • Progress. Whether each area is on programme, and what is blocking it.
  • Materials and housekeeping. Storage, protection, waste and clear routes.
  • Actions. Who fixes what, by when, with a photo as evidence.

This is a general starting point, not a compliance document. Check it against your contract, your company procedures and local regulations (for example OSHA in the US or CDM in the UK).

Daily, weekly and pre-handover inspections

  • Daily walk-round. A short check of active areas for safety, housekeeping and blockers.
  • Weekly inspection. The full checklist, area by area, with progress and quality.
  • Pre-handover (snag or punch list). A detailed check of finished work for defects before handover. “Snag list” is the UK, Gulf and Australian term; “punch list” is the North American one.

How to run an inspection with photos as evidence

  1. Inspect one area at a time and record the area name on every item.
  2. Photograph every action item, and good work that is about to be covered.
  3. Write one sentence per issue: what, where, and who needs to act.
  4. Carry open actions into the next daily report until they are closed.

Questions

What should a construction site inspection checklist include?

Access and boundary, housekeeping, working at height, excavations, electrics, plant, PPE and welfare, fire and emergency, workmanship, progress, materials, work before covering, actions and a sign-off.

How often should site inspections be done?

A short daily walk-round of active areas plus a full weekly inspection is a common pattern; your contract, company procedures and local regulations set the minimum.

Who carries out construction site inspections?

Usually the site manager, site engineer or supervisor, with safety advisers, clients’ representatives and building inspectors carrying out their own inspections.

What is the difference between a snag list and a punch list?

Nothing but the name. Both list defects and unfinished items to fix before handover; snag list is used in the UK, Gulf and Australia, punch list in North America.
