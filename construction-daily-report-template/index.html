Construction daily report template.

A construction daily report (also called a daily log or site diary) records what happened on site each day: weather, who was there, work completed, deliveries, inspections, safety, delays and instructions. Use the free template below on paper, or keep the same record on your iPhone with Projomia’s Daily Log, which works offline.

Why the daily report matters

The daily report is the project’s memory. It explains why work slipped, supports claims and variations, shows who was on site, and lets anyone who missed a day catch up in minutes. Written the same day, with photographs, it is the most reliable record a project has.

What to include

  1. Project and date. Name, number, location, today’s phase of work and who wrote the report.
  2. Weather. Conditions and temperature, plus any hours lost.
  3. Workforce. Each trade and subcontractor, with headcount and hours.
  4. Work completed. Activities by area or level, with progress in quantities or percent.
  5. Delays and blockers. What is waiting, why, and who needs to act. This is the line most reports leave out and most disputes depend on.
  6. Materials, deliveries and equipment. What arrived, what is short, which plant was used.
  7. Inspections, safety and visitors. Results, hazards, incidents, and who came to site.
  8. Instructions and changes. Anything you were told to do differently, and by whom.
  9. Photos. Progress and evidence photographs linked to the entries above.
  10. Tomorrow and sign-off. Planned work, open decisions, and the responsible person’s signature.

Site diary, daily log or daily report?

They are the same record under different names. “Site diary” is common in the UK, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand; “daily log” and “daily report” are common in North America and the Middle East. Whatever you call it, the content above is what makes it useful.

Site engineer daily progress report (DPR) format

On many sites, especially in India and the Gulf, the daily report is called the DPR (daily progress report). It is compiled by the site engineer at the end of the shift and becomes the basis for the weekly report. A practical DPR format uses the same fields as the template above, with three habits that make it more useful:

  • One format for every subcontractor, so reports can be combined without retyping.
  • Progress against the plan: each activity with today’s quantity, cumulative quantity and percent complete, matched to the programme or bill of quantities.
  • Photos tagged by floor or zone, referenced from the activity they prove.

Check your contract for any required format or sign-off.

Questions

What should a construction daily report include?

Project and date, weather, workforce by trade, work completed, delays and blockers, materials and deliveries, equipment, inspections, safety, visitors, instructions or changes, photographs, tomorrow’s plan and a sign-off.

Who writes the daily report?

Usually the site manager, site engineer, foreman or superintendent, on the same day, while the details are fresh.

Is a site diary the same as a daily log?

Yes. Site diary, daily log and daily report are regional names for the same daily record of work on site.

How long should daily reports be kept?

Keep them at least for the life of the project and its defects or warranty period. Many firms keep them for years in case of claims; check your contract and local law.

What is a DPR in construction?

A DPR (daily progress report) is the site engineer’s end-of-shift record of work done, manpower, materials, equipment and issues, measured against the plan. It is the same record as a daily report or site diary.
